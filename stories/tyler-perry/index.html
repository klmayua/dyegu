Tyler Perry

Tyler Perry saved $12,000 as a bill collector and spent every dollar renting a hall in Atlanta in 1992. He expected 1,200 people; about thirty showed up. Six foot five, sleeping in a Geo Metro he hid from the repo man, he kept staging the play once a year while it failed — until 1998 broke everything open.

Inside the wilderness & turning point

Documented turning point

“He bet his entire $12,000 savings on one play.”

He expected 1,200 people. About 30 came. He lost his apartment.

Documented turning point

“Six foot five. Living in a Geo Metro.”

Three months in that car, hiding it from the repo man, while the same play failed every year.

Documented turning point

“He bought 330 acres of an army base.”

$30 million in 2015, about $250 million invested, 12 soundstages. Warner Bros.' lot is 110 acres.

The DYEGU Rule · Takeaway for your own road

“He didn't change the dream. He changed how long he was willing to chase it, and he kept learning while he chased it. Six years of the same play failing, then year seven. That's the DYEGU rule.”
