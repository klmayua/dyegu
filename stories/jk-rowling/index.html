J.K. Rowling

In the mid-1990s Joanne Rowling was a single mother on state benefits in Edinburgh, still grieving her mother, with the opening chapters of a children's book. Publisher after publisher said no. Then an eight-year-old girl read chapter one.

Three moments that mattered

Inside the wilderness & turning point

Documented turning point

“Twelve publishers said no. An eight-year-old said yes.”

Bloomsbury's chairman gave chapter one to his daughter Alice. An hour later she came downstairs asking for more.

Documented turning point

“The first print run of Harry Potter: 500 copies.”

Three years earlier its author was on income support in Edinburgh, "as poor as it is possible to be in modern Britain."

Documented turning point

“"I nearly died."”

A single mother on benefits a few years earlier, she heard Scholastic had won the US rights for $105,000.

The DYEGU Rule · Takeaway for your own road

“Rock bottom strips away everything you were pretending to be. What's left is what you build on. Jo Rowling had one unfinished book and a baby asleep in a buggy. She built on that. That's the DYEGU rule.”

Question to sit with: Tell me in the comments: what's the one thing you'd still build on, even at your lowest?
